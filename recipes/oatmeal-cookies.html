Instructions

  1. Melt or brown the butter 5 min

    Melted butter: melt in the microwave or in a small pan, then let it cool until it is no longer hot to the touch.

    Browned butter (better): melt it in a light-coloured skillet over medium heat, swirling occasionally so it melts evenly. It foams, then the colour goes from pale yellow to golden to a light brown — a minute or two. Take it off the heat the moment it smells nutty, pour it into a heatproof bowl and cool it.

    Cool to the touch before it goes into the dough: hot butter will start to cook the eggs. Browned butter loses a little water, so the dough is marginally firmer — no adjustment needed.

  2. Heat the oven and whisk the dry ingredients 160 °C · 4 min

    Heat the oven to 160 °C (325 °F, no fan) and line a baking sheet with parchment or a silicone mat. In a medium bowl, whisk of flour with of baking soda, of fine sea salt, of cinnamon and of cardamom. Set aside.

  3. Whisk the wet ingredients 2 min

    In a large bowl, whisk of packed light brown sugar and of granulated sugar with of beaten egg, of yolk, of vanilla and the cooled butter until smooth and slightly glossy.

  4. Bring the dough together 3 min

    Switch to a large rubber spatula. Add the flour mixture in three parts, stirring after each until the streaks disappear. Fold in of rolled oats, then of mix-ins. Stop as soon as everything is combined — over-mixing makes the cookies tough.

  5. Chill, if you have the time 30 min

    Optional but worth it. Rest the dough in the fridge for 30 minutes (or up to 3 days): the oats hydrate, and the cookies bake up thicker, chewier and more flavourful. Straight from the bowl they spread a little more.

  6. Scoop and space 10 min

    Roll the dough into balls of about each — from this batch — and set them on the lined sheet, 5 cm apart to leave room for spreading. Press each ball down gently with your fingers so it spreads evenly instead of doming.

  7. Bake 9–15 min · 160 °C

    Bake one sheet at a time, on the middle shelf, for 9 to 15 minutes, turning the sheet once halfway. They are done when the edges are light golden and the middles still look almost wet — they firm up as they cool. Bigger balls need a couple of minutes more.

  8. Cool 5 min + rack

    Leave them on the sheet for 5 minutes to set, then move them to a rack to cool completely. They are at their best still slightly warm, and they keep for days after that.

Why the egg is in grams

  • Scaling by cookie count does not land on whole eggs, and “1.4 eggs” is not an instruction — so eggs are weighed like everything else.
  • Beat a whole egg, then weigh out the amount the page asks for. Whisk in a little of the yolk for the rest.
  • The yolk is there for chew: it is roughly a quarter of an egg's weight and it is worth not skipping.
  • For a very small batch, cracking one egg and weighing ⅔ of it is the fiddly part — the payoff is a batch that fits a single sheet.

Storing, freezing, and substitutions

  • Baked: airtight for 3 days at room temperature, or a week in the fridge.
  • Dough: up to 3 days in the fridge, or scoop into balls, freeze until hard, bag them and keep for 3 months. Bake from frozen, adding a couple of minutes.
  • Coconut oil: swap in the same weight, then rest the dough for 20 minutes so the oats absorb it. Unrefined for coconut flavour, refined for none.
  • Vegan: vegan butter or coconut oil, and a flax egg in place of both egg and yolk — of flaxseed meal whisked with of water, rested 5–10 minutes.
  • Mix-ins: raisins, dried fruit, chopped nuts or chocolate, all by weight, so swap freely.
