Instructions

  1. Brine the cabbage 12 h

    Dissolve of salt in of cold water. Submerge the quartered cabbage. Brine for 12 hours, flipping the quarters once or twice so they salt evenly.

    Brine strength is fixed at 65 g salt per 1 L water. If the cabbage is not fully submerged, mix extra brine at that same ratio and pour it in.

  2. Drain and rinse 5 min

    Strain and discard the brine. Rinse the cabbage 2–3 times with cold water. Taste a piece — it should be pleasantly salty, not overpowering. If it is too salty, rinse once more.

  3. Squeeze and drain 30 min

    Gently squeeze the cabbage to remove excess water. Place it cut-side down in a colander and drain for 30 minutes. This is the key to a less watery kimchi.

  4. Make the rice paste 800 W · 2 min

    Put of dry rice and of water in a microwave-safe bowl. Microwave at 800 W for 2 minutes, then rest covered for 5 minutes. If it is still hard, add a splash of water and cook in 30-second bursts. Blend until smooth and glue-like, then cool completely.

    Using gochujang? Add (about 15 mL per 2 kg) — only if you want extra depth and your paste is not already complete.

  6. Coat the cabbage 10 min

    Spread the paste onto every leaf of the brined cabbage. Work it into the layers, especially near the core.

  7. Pack tightly 10 min

    Pack the kimchi into clean glass jars or food-grade containers — about of capacity for this batch. Press down firmly to remove air pockets and keep the cabbage submerged in its own juices.

  8. Ferment 3 days · 18–24 °C

    Leave at room temperature (18–24 °C) for 3 days. Press the kimchi down once daily to release gas and keep it submerged.

  9. Refrigerate 2–3 days

    Move to the fridge for at least 2–3 days before eating. It keeps fermenting slowly and gets more sour over time.

If you already packed it without garlic or ginger

No problem — it is not ruined.

  • Open the jars and tip the kimchi into a bowl.
  • Mix in of grated garlic and of grated ginger.
  • Repack and press down firmly, then carry on fermenting as normal.

For a less watery kimchi

  • Squeeze the brined cabbage well — then once more.
  • Squeeze the grated carrot dry, ideally in a clean towel.
  • Use the rice paste as a binder; it holds the paste to the leaves.
  • Press the kimchi down daily during room-temperature fermentation.
  • Do not add extra liquid unless the kimchi is not submerged.

Salt, and why the ratio matters

  • Salt is the only safety-critical number here, so it never goes below 65 g per 1 L water.
  • Do not taste-tune the brine upward on a whim: over-salted cabbage is hard to rescue, and rinsing only removes surface salt.
  • Taste the cabbage after rinsing, not before.
  • Fermenting jars should stay submerged and smell sour and garlicky. Fizzy is fine; fuzzy, slimy or rotten-smelling is not — discard it.
