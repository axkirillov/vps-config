Instructions

  1. Put on gloves, then chop 10 min

    Wear gloves. Roughly chop of hot red or orange chilies, of red bell peppers, and of garlic.

    Gloves are not optional. Capsaicin stays on skin for hours and transfers to eyes and faces. Do not touch your face while chopping, and keep your hands away from your eyes even after washing.

    Buying by weight instead: of chilies, of red bell pepper, of garlic.

  2. Dilute the vinegar essence 2 min

    Never add water to the essence — always essence to water. The reverse can spit and boil over. Work in a ventilated spot, keep the bottle away from your face, and rinse any splash with plenty of water.

  3. Combine in a pot 1 min

    Put the diluted vinegar, the chopped hot chilies, the bell peppers, the garlic and of kosher salt into a small pot. Use a non-reactive pot — stainless steel, enamel or glass, not bare aluminium or unlined copper.

  4. Simmer until tender 7–10 min

    Bring to a simmer over medium-high heat. Reduce the heat to low, cover, and simmer until the peppers are tender, 7–10 minutes.

    Open a window and turn on the extractor fan. Simmering chilies release capsaicin into the air. Do not lean over the pot or inhale the vapours — it burns the throat and makes eyes stream from across the room.

  5. Blend smooth 2 min

    Transfer everything to a blender and purée until completely smooth. Take the small centre cap out of the blender lid and cover that opening with a folded towel so steam can escape.

    Hot liquid in a sealed blender builds pressure and blows the lid off. Vent it, and start on the lowest speed.

  6. Cool, then stand for 3 days 3 days

    Pour into a jar and let it cool uncovered. Cover tightly and refrigerate for 3 days to let the flavours meld before you judge it.

    Fresh sauce tastes raw and sharp on day one. The garlic and chile settle into each other over those three days — this is the step that makes it taste finished. Time in the fridge does not change with batch size.

  7. Store fridge

    Keep it in the refrigerator. It will hold for several weeks or longer.

Vinegar essence safety

  • Never use it undiluted. At 25% and above it will burn skin and tissue on contact.
  • Essence into water, always this order. Water into essence can flash-boil and spray.
  • Labelled by percentage, not volume. If your bottle says "25 ml" or the strength is not printed, stop here and read it again — the amounts below assume a percentage figure.
  • Different strength, different split. Pick your percentage above and the essence and water amounts recalculate to the same final acidity.
  • Glass or plastic only. Dilute in glass or plastic, not metal, and keep it away from your face and eyes.

If anything is unclear on the label, use ready-made 5% vinegar instead — it is the same thing the dilution produces, and it removes every hazard above.

Heat, and how to steer it

  • All the heat comes from the hot chilies. The bell peppers add volume, colour and sweetness, and no heat at all.
  • Hotter: leave the seeds and pith in the hot chilies, or add an extra chili beyond the amount above.
  • Milder: core out the seeds and white pith first, or swap some hot chilies for more bell pepper.
  • Minder: a sauce that tastes flat, not hot, usually needs more salt rather than more chili — taste it after the three days, not before.

Troubleshooting

  • Too thick to pour: loosen with a spoonful of water or 5% vinegar — not with fresh essence.
  • Separating in the jar: normal, especially with a lot of red bell pepper. Shake before use.
  • Bitter or harsh: usually the garlic or the seeds. Give it the full 3 days first; if it persists, add a pinch of salt.
  • Fizzy, mouldy, or smelling of rot: discard the jar. Fermentation fizz belongs in the pickle recipe, not here.
  • Colour dulling: it darkens as it sits and oxidises. Harmless.

Storage

  • This is a fridge sauce, not a shelf-stable preserve. Acid, salt and cold are what hold it; take away the cold and you take away the shelf life.
  • Keep it in the fridge, tightly covered, for several weeks or longer.
  • Use a clean spoon every time, and smell it before you use it — a sauce that has turned sharp or fizzy is done.
  • For gifting or long storage, a hot-water-bath process and pH testing are a different recipe, and not what this page describes.
