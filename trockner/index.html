Heat-pump tumble dryers under 500 € with a Stiftung Warentest verdict of “gut”

Grades and measurements: primary source — the purchased Stiftung Warentest issues 9/2023, 9/2024, 9/2025 and 9/2026.
Prices & shipping: retail listings checked 20 October 2026, 15:30–16:30 CEST. Manuals: ./manuals/.
Five machines clear both hurdles — and the cheapest one to buy is not the cheapest to own Beko B3T4224SD (gut 2,0, 499 €) wins on ten-year cost; Beko B3T41230 (gut 2,2, 499 €) costs 1 327 € over ten years because it burns 1,44 kWh per full cotton cycle — the thirstiest figure printed in the 9/2024 test. The energy label is measured in the cotton cupboard-dry programme at full and half load, which since the 2025 ecodesign rules is the machine’s Eco programme — Stiftung Warentest now writes it as “Baumwolle schranktrocken (Eco-Programm)”.

What the test issues say about these five in words

  • B3T4224SD — “sehr gut” for cotton cupboard-dry at full load, the fastest cotton programme in its field (131 min), full marks (1,0) for safety and build. Its weakness is the half-load run (only “befriedigend”).
  • B3T42240S — the 9/2023 “Preistipp”, still “gut (2,2)” on re-test in 9/2024. Drying 1,8 is genuinely good; the marks are lost in handling (2,7) — the foundation criticised the rest-run indicator as unreliable on this family.
  • B3T42249S — the honest weak spot: “befriedigend” (2,8) for drying, i.e. the core job, the weakest of the five. It is cheap, not good at drying.
  • B3T41230 — dries well (1,8) and is built well (1,3), but 1,44 kWh per full cotton cycle is the highest figure printed in the 9/2024 test and drives 828 € of electricity over ten years — more than the purchase price.
  • Gorenje DHNE83 — good drying (2,2) and very good environmental marks, but at maximum load the door sprang open during the cycle (the test docked half a grade for it), and handling is only “befriedigend” (3,3): rest-run display “ausreichend”, loading “ausreichend”.

3 · Which setting is used to measure energy efficiency?

One answer, in three layers.

(a) The legal measuring programme: cotton, cupboard-dry, full and partial load

Since Regulation (EU) 392/2012 the declared consumption, the energy class and the condensation efficiency of a dryer all refer to the standard cotton programme, measured to EN 61121 at full and at half load. The manuals name the button combination exactly:

Beko — footnote 3 of the technical data table (p. 51 in the B3T4224SD manual, p. 49 in the B3T42249S manual, p. 50 in the B3T41230 manual):
“Baumwoll-Schranktrockenprogramm bei vollständiger und partieller Beladung ist das Standardtrocknungsprogramm, auf den sich die Informationen am Etikett und im Datenblatt beziehen; dieses Programm ist zum Trocknen normaler feuchter Baumwollwäsche geeignet; dabei handelt es sich das effizienteste Programm im Hinblick auf Energieverbrauch bei Baumwolle.”
“Baumwoll-Schranktrockenprogramm” is not a button — and that is why you cannot find it No dryer in this list has a programme with that name. “Baumwoll-Schranktrockenprogramm” is the regulation’s term for the combination of a cotton programme and the cupboard-dry level, and Beko never prints “Baumwolle” on the panel at all: the cotton programme is called “Koch-/Buntwäsche”, its eco variant “Koch-/Buntwäsche Öko”, and the dryness is a separate button. The phrase itself appears exactly once in the whole manual, in footnote 3 of the technical data table at the very back — not in the programme table.

(b) Since 1 July 2025 that programme is the machine’s Eco programme

Regulation (EU) 2023/2533 kept the cotton-cupboard-dry logic but moved the reference cycle onto the dedicated Eco cotton programme. Stiftung Warentest adopted the same wording in the 9/2026 issue — in its downgrade rules it writes literally:

“Ab Befriedigend für das Trockenprogramm Baumwolle schranktrocken, volle Beladung (Eco-Programm) werteten wir das Urteil Trocknen ab.” — Stiftung Warentest, “So haben wir getestet”, test 9/2026

and the Gorenje manual spells out why the Eco button is the one that counts:

Gorenje (DHNE83), programme table:
“Das Programm Eco eignet sich zum Trocknen von feuchter Wäsche aus Baumwolle und ist hinsichtlich des Energieverbrauchs … das effizienteste Programm. *Das Programm Eco wird verwendet, um die Einhaltung der EU-Gesetzgebung für Ökodesign zu prüfen 2023/2533.”

(c) What the test actually does (and why the test ≠ the label)

Stiftung Warentest measures consumption in that same cotton programme — the table column “Stromverbrauch gemessen” is the Eco/cotton cupboard-dry cycle at full load — but the grade also contains four other cotton/easy-care runs, evenness, programme duration, handling, noise, refrigerant and build. That is how a machine can be class C and still score 2,5, or class E and still score 2,2.

Why one dryer is “A+++” in the manual and “C” on the shop page The 2025 rescaling abolished A+++ and A++. The same appliance that carried A+++ (177 kWh/a) is now class C; the B3T41230’s old A++ is now E. The label letters in the test table above are the ones the manufacturers declared at the time of each test — which is why 2023/2024 issues show A++/A+++ and the 2025/2026 issues show B/C/E for comparable hardware.

(d) “But we’d never use the Eco programme” — is the label meaningless then?

Partly. Two different claims hide in that sentence, and only one survives contact with the numbers.

Claim 1: “we’d press the normal cotton programme instead, so Eco is just the slow pious option.” Not on these machines. Beko prints its own consumption table (B3T4224SD manual, p. 32), all rows at 8 kg, 1400 rpm, 50 % residual moisture:

On this dryer the Eco programme is both cheaper and 35 minutes faster than the normal cotton cupboard-dry setting — because “normal cotton, Schranktrocken” is not the same cycle: it over-dries a little and runs longer. So “the Eco programme costs you an hour” is false here; what it does cost you is the free choice of dryness level, because the Öko programme is locked to Schranktrocken.

Claim 2: “we’d dry small loads, synthetics, or need things bone-dry.” That one is true, and it is the important one. The label figure is a full-load cotton best case. If your real week is 2 kg of mixed shirts, the eco cotton cycle is the wrong tool — Pflegeleicht at 4 kg delivers the best kWh per kg of the whole table (0,138), and Express is the worst (0,750 kWh/kg, seven times the eco cycle) even though it looks like the frugal choice.

“Das Zeitsparprogramm des Hanseatic-Modells klingt praktischer, als es ist: Es lief gut ein Drittel kürzer als im Eco-Baumwollprogramm, verbrauchte aber etwas mehr Energie – und die Wäsche kam zu feucht aus der Trommel.” — test 9/2026

Practical reading: use Eco/Öko for full cotton loads (the default, and the cheapest per kg), Pflegeleicht for synthetics, and reach for Schranktrocken+/Extratrocken or Express only when the laundry actually demands it. What you should not do is compare two dryers by their label class and then run neither of them in that programme — the label ranks machines, it does not predict your bill.

8 · Method and caveats

  • Grades, sub-grades, consumption, durations, classes: read directly from the four test booklets. Where a model was re-tested, both verdicts are given.
  • Prices: live retailer listings and the Geizhals/heise offer feed on 20 Oct 2026, plus the “mittlerer Preis” printed in each test for comparison. Large-appliance prices move weekly; the timestamps are the only thing that makes them meaningful.
  • Shipping: Amazon 14,90 €; MediaMarkt free for direct sales during the current promotion (marketplace sellers vary and are flagged); expert.de charges 79,90 € for the B3T4224SD. Installation, floor delivery and old-appliance removal are extra everywhere.
  • Ten-year cost: purchase price + Stiftung Warentest’s 10-year electricity model. It is a model, not a bill — it assumes 1 000 kg/year, which is a family of four, not a two-person household.
  • Borderline: the B3T42249S (476,99 €) and the marketplace B3T41230 (499,00 €) sit on the line; the B3TSW48C9ZW sits 5 € above it and would otherwise be the best buy.
  • Model traps: B3T4224SD ≠ B3T42249S ≠ B3T42240S ≠ B3T422391 ≠ B3TSW48C9ZW. Beko’s codes differ by drum generation and year, and only some carry the “gut” verdict. The Gorenje is DHNE83 in the test and DHNE83/AT or DHNE82 in some shops.
